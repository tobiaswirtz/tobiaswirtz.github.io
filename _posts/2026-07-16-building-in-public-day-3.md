Day 3. The list was cold outreach, a Business Model Canvas, and one customer interview. All three got done. The interview is the reason I'm writing this post at all.

## Cold outreach: sent, silent

I sent cold emails to the companies from yesterday's qualified list. No responses yet. Too early to read anything into it, but worth writing down that the count so far is zero.

## Lean Canvas

I built a Lean Canvas to force myself to be precise about what I'm building, who it's for, and how the money works. Putting it on one page made the weak spots obvious.

The biggest open question that fell out of it is **channels**. I have "personal outreach" and then a question mark. I know how to reach a handful of companies through my own network and one cold list. I do not yet have a repeatable way to reach the German Mittelstand at scale. Everything else on the canvas can be right and this one gap still sinks it.

## The interview that reshaped the plan

I did one interview with a Mittelstand CEO. Three things came out of it, in increasing order of how much they changed my thinking.

**First, the need is real.** Documentation is genuinely required in his world, and today they do it with slower, more manual mechanisms. That's hypothesis 1 getting a little more solid: the pain exists and they already spend effort on it.

**Second, on-premise is not a nice-to-have, it's the gate.** He is currently turning down every cloud AI vendor that lands on his desk, because his crown-jewel IP cannot leave the building. This is sharper than I had it. I'd been treating "EU-hosted" as the differentiator, but for this segment EU hosting alone won't clear the bar. Self-hosted, on his own hardware, is the price of even being considered.

**Third, and this is the one that's rearranging the whole thing: the hard part isn't the tech.** People resist documenting because they're afraid it makes them replaceable, sometimes to the point of reporting false numbers so their process stays a black box. So the real job isn't a better transcription model or a cleaner voice stack. It's trust. It's giving the person on the shop floor a reason to actually buy into being interviewed. A perfect AI interviewer that people quietly sabotage is worth nothing.

## Hypotheses

1. **Knowledge-loss pain, pay five figures once** — small positive signal. The need was confirmed; willingness to pay the specific number is still untested.
2. Let an AI talk to employees — no new data, but the interview points at *why* this one is hard: the resistance is about job security, not technology.

The interview also surfaced something my six hypotheses didn't name explicitly: **on-premise is a hard gate for this segment, not a feature.** I'm promoting that to a first-class assumption to test.

## Tomorrow

1. Rework the pitch and product framing around trust and buy-in, not transcription quality.
2. Attack the channel question head-on: find one repeatable way to reach Mittelstand companies at scale.
3. Do the second interview and specifically probe the on-premise gate and the fear-of-replaceability dynamic.
